What a school website needs

Parents researching a school want a clear picture of the programs and the people. These are the website elements a school site usually carries.

Programs and approach

A plain explanation of what students learn and how you teach helps families judge whether your school suits their child.

Teachers and staff

Parents want to know who will teach their child. Profiles of staff humanize the school.

Important dates and information

Term dates, open days and key announcements, written on the page, answer the questions families ask most.

Contact and visits

Office hours, a phone number, an email and a note on how to arrange a visit help interested families take the next step.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should a school website include?

The programs or grades you offer, your approach to teaching, staff profiles, key dates, how to contact the school and how to arrange a visit. Many schools also add a page for current parents.

Can I build a school website without coding?

Yes. You describe the school in plain language and review what your AI coworker builds. You do not write code or design pages yourself.

Can the site include parent logins, forms or calendar sync?

This page does not promise parent or student portals, logins, calendar sync or forms. Your site can present information and contact details. If you need those features, or have accessibility or procurement requirements, ask your AI coworker what it can build and check with your school or district.

How many credits does a school website use?

Ndovesha uses credits. You can try it free with 200 credits, and paid plans include monthly credits (the Starter plan includes 1,000). How many credits your site uses depends on what you ask for. See the pricing page.
